U.S. university holidays and start times

The United States is a federal country and does not have a government department like China's Ministry of Education that manages higher education; instead, national and regional accreditation centers recognize institutions and majors. The academic system also varies by region and school. The academic calendar system adopted by American universities varies by school and can be further divided into four types: Early Semesters, Semesters, Quarters, and Trimesters. Different academic systems affect factors such as credit calculation, course offering, and study cycles. In the United States, the early semester system is becoming increasingly popular, and many schools have switched from the semester and semester system to the early semester system.

1. Semester System: This is the most widely adopted school system. In the traditional semester system, the nine-month academic year is divided into two semesters of sixteen weeks, with some schools offering semesters as long as eighteen weeks. Typically, each semester consists of about fifteen weeks of teaching and one week of final exams. The first semester starts at the end of August, usually with an orientation week for new students, ending in mid to late December of the same year. The second semester starts in early January and ends in late May. After the official semester ends, students are free to take summer courses called Summersessions.

For example, Yale University is a typical school that implements a semester system. During the 2010-2011 academic year, dormitories opened on August 25, 2010, semester began on September 1, final exams started from December 11 to 18, and official vacation started on December 19; The spring semester started on January 10, 2011, with spring break from March 4 to 21, and final exams from May 3 to 11. The summer vacation officially started on May 23. After that came the three-month summer vacation, during which many students used the summer for internships, part-time jobs, or to earn summer credits.

Credits are calculated on a semester basis (Semester credit), with one hour of class per week counting as one semester credit (1Semester credit or 1 Semester hour). Typically, a course lasts three hours per week, and students can earn three credits. In semester schools, students generally need 120-128 semester credits to apply for graduation.

2. Quarter/Term System: Most universities in California use the quarter system, and some schools in the East also use this system, such as Dartmouth College and Drexel mentioned below. The quarter system divides the annual curriculum into four seasons: spring, summer, autumn, and winter, with each term lasting only two and a half months (ten to twelve weeks, and about eight to ten weeks in summer), making the curriculum very compact. Foreign students attending institutions with this system must attend at least three academic seasons per academic year (paying tuition three times) to maintain student status, and if they do not register in any semester outside the summer, they must report to the school authorities. The summer class offers regular courses, but they are elective. Quarter system One hour of classes per week counts as one quarter credit (1 Quarter creditor, 1 quarter hour). On average, students earn 14-18 semester credits per semester, and generally need 180-192 semester credits to apply for graduation. Due to the short class hours and frequent exams (one month of study leads to midterms, another month leads to finals), students must immediately get into the situation at the start of the semester and must also be able to adapt to continuous stress.

The calculation method for semester credits from semester credits is that typically, four semester credits can be used to count toward three semester credits. The conversion method is roughly as follows:

Term credits multiplied by 0.667 = semester credits (semesterunits)

Quarter credits multiplied by 1.5 = quarter units

For example, another Ivy League university, Dartmouth, uses a typical quarterly system, dividing the year into four terms, each term lasting 10 weeks. Typically, students take 3 courses per semester. Students complete 35 courses to graduate. Interestingly, Dartmouth's graduation requirements include a requirement to complete a 50-yard swim test, but that's a side ^_^ note

Fall semester 2010: Semester starts on September 22, exams start on December 4, exams end on December 8, and the holiday begins;

Winter semester 2011: School starts January 4, exams start March 12, exams end on March 16, official holiday;

Spring semester 2011: School starts on March 28, exams start on June 3, exams end on June 7, and the official holiday begins.

3. Trimester System: The three-semester system divides the twelve-month academic year into three equal parts, each lasting sixteen weeks. The first three semesters (fall classes) start at the end of August or early September and end before Christmas Day (December 25). The second three semesters (winter classes) start in early or mid-January and end in April. The third three semesters start in late April or early May and end in August. The third three semesters are divided into two terms: spring (April to June) and summer (June to August). Credits are divided into semester units. Institutions using this system are fewer in the United States, while more in Europe, New Zealand, and Australia. Institutions adopting this system require foreign students to attend at least two semesters each year, including autumn and winter semesters, while spring and summer classes are optional. There are four registration opportunities per year, so students enrolled in this system have a chance to graduate early.

Schools currently using a three-semester system include Northwestern Polytechnic University

4. Early Semester System: The early semester system divides the academic year (nine months) into two units of fifteen to eighteen weeks, with the first semester (autumn class) slightly shorter than the second semester (spring session). The first semester starts at the end of August and ends around December 20. The second semester starts in mid-January and ends in mid-May, with optional summer courses (Summersession). Credit calculation and graduation credit requirements are the same as those for the semester system.

In recent years, many schools have adopted Early Semester, and currently, schools using the early semester system include the University of Southern California. The popular program for Chinese students is the USC Master of Accounting, which offers both summer and fall enrollment. For students lacking a background in prerequisite courses, they can start their studies in summer to make up for the prerequisites.

However, regardless of the American university system, most schools start the academic year in the fallterm. Although some schools accept applications at any semester, the fall class offers a wide range of foundational courses and also holds information sessions for new students. Additionally, most scholarships target students applying for fall admission, so the fall term should be the best choice for admission.

美国是联邦制国家，没有一个像中国教育部这样的政府部门管理其高等教育，而由国家和地区的认证中心来认可高等院校和专业。学制上也因地域和学校而有不同。美国大学所采行的学历(AcademicCalendarSystem)因学校而异，可细分为早学期制(EarlySemesters)、学期制(Semesters)、学季制(Quarters)、三学期制(Trimester)等四种。不同的学制会影响毕业学分的计算、开课方式及修业周期等学习因素。在美国，早学期制越来越受到欢迎，许多学校也都从学季及学期制转入早学期制。

1、学期制(Semester)：是最多学校采用的学制。在传统的学期制内，九个月的学年共分成两个长达十六个星期的学期，也有一些学校提供长达十八个星期的学期。通常每学期约有十五个星期的教学期及一个星期的期末考。第一个学期在8月底开学，一般对新生会有个orientationweek,当年的12月中下旬结束。第二个学期于一月初开学，5月下旬结束。正式学期结束后学生还可自由选修暑期课程〈Summersession〉。

比如耶鲁大学就是典型的实行学期制的学校。2010-2011学年期间，2010年8月25日宿舍开放，9月1日开学，12月11日-18日期末考试，12月19日正式放假;春季学期从2011年1月10日开始，3月4日-21日放春假，5月3日-11日为期末考试时间。5月23日正式放暑假。之后就是长达3个月的大暑假，很多学生会利用暑假实习、打工或者修读暑期学分。

学分计算为学期制(Semestercredit)，每周上课一小时为一个学期学分(1Semestercreditor1Semesterhour)。通常一门课一周上课三小时，学生可获取三个学分。在学期制的学校，一般学生需要120-128个学期学分才可以申请毕业。

2、学季制(Quarter/Term)：加州的大学大部分采用学季制，此外，东部的一些学校也采用此种学制，比如下面提到的Dartmouthcollege,Drexel等大学。学季制将一年的课程分为春、夏、秋、冬等四季，每一季一学期(term)，每学期仅两个半月(十至十二周，夏季约八至十周)，课程十分紧凑。就读采此类制度院校的外国学生，每学年至少需就读三个学季(付三次的学费)方能维持学生身分，且若不在夏季以外的任一学季注册，均须向学校当局报备。夏季班提供正规课程，但为选修性质。学季制每周上课一小时为一学季学分(1Quartercreditor1Quarterhour)。学生平均每个学季念14-18个学季学分，一般需要180-192个学季学分才可以申请毕业。由于每季上课时间短迫，考试频频(念一个月就进入期中考，再念一个月，就进入期末考)，学生一开学就得立即进入状况，而且也须能适应持续性的紧张压力。

学季学分对学期学分的计算方式，通常四个学季学分可以用来抵三个学期学分，转换方式大致如下：

学季学分乘以0.667=学期学分(semesterunits)

学期学分乘以1.5=学季学分(quarterunits)

比如另一所常春藤名校达特茅斯大学就采用了典型的学季制学历，将全年分为四个学季，每个学季(Term)是10周，一般来讲，一个学季会选3门课。学生修满35门课即可毕业。有意思的是，在达特茅斯的毕业要求里，有一项：必须完成50码游泳测验，这是题外话^_^

2010年夏季学期：6月24日开学，8月25日课程结束，8月28日开始考试，8月31日考试结束;

2010年秋季学期：9月22日开学，12月4日开始考试，12月8日考试结束，开始放假;

2011年冬季学期：1月4日开学，3月12日开始考试，3月16日考试结束，正式放假;

2011年春季学期：3月28日开学，6月3日开始考试，6月7日考试结束，正式放假。

3、三学期制(Trimester)：三学期制将十二个月的学年分为三等份，为期十六星期长的学期，第一个三学期(秋季班)于八月底或九月初开学，圣诞节〈十二月二十五日〉前结束。第二个三学期(冬季班)于一月初或中旬开学，四月结束。第三个三学期从四月底或五月初开课，八月结束。第三个三学期共分为两个小学期(Terms)，即春季班(四月至六月)与夏季班(六月至八月)。学分为学期制(semesterunit)。采用这种制度的院校在美国较少，欧洲与纽西兰、澳洲较多。采用这类课程的院校要求外国学生每年至少要在秋季与冬季两学期就读，而春夏季班则可自由选修。一年共有四次注册机会，所以就读此学制的学生有机会提早毕业。

目前使用三学期制的学校例如：NorthwesternPolytechnicUniversity

4、早学期制(EarlySemester)：早学期制将一学年(九个月)分成两个十五至十八个星期的的单位，第一个学期(秋季班)比第二个学期(春季班)稍微短一些。第一个学期在八月底开始至大约十二月二十日左右结束。第二个学期在一月中旬开课，五月中旬结束，可自由选修暑期课程〈Summersession〉。学分计算与毕业学分的要求与学期制一样。

近几年较多的学校采EarlySemester，目前使用早学期制的学校例如：UniversityofSouthernCalifornia。中国学生申请的大热门，南加大的会计硕士课程，就有夏季和秋季两季入学。针对一些缺乏比较多先修课背景的学生，可以在夏季开始学习，来补足先修课。

不过，不管是哪种美国大学学制，一般的学校均以秋季(Fallterm)为一学年的起始，虽然有些学校在任何学期都接受入学申请，但是秋季班开学时有较多的基础课程可供选择，并且也会为新生举办说明会，此外，大多数奖学金都会针对申请秋季入学的学生，所以，秋季班应为入学的较佳选择。
