Milan is Italy's second largest city and the largest industrial and financial center. It is located in the western part of the Po River basin. Milan was founded in the 4th century BC and became an industrial and commercial center in the 9th century. It was the capital of the Republic of Milan in the 14th and 15th centuries. It was incorporated into Italy in 1861. It is now the most economically strong city in the country. Milan is the largest transportation hub in northern Italy, with advanced railways, highways, and river transport.

Milan's city preserves the circular layout built in the Middle Ages, with wide streets, numerous squares, ancient castles, and winding alleys. The Dumo Cathedral is Gothic and one of Europe's most famous churches, where Napoleon was crowned in 1805. The Ambrosia Library is a treasure trove for rare books and ancient manuscripts. The Vittorio Emanuele II Gallery is cruciform and built in 1887, where Milan's top shops are concentrated. La Scala is the largest in the country and is known as the "Mecca of Opera."

School Introduction

Founded in 1923, the University of Milan is one of Italy's largest and most renowned public universities, located in Milan, the capital of the Lombardy province in northern Italy—the second largest city in Italy. Milan is a hub for international enterprises and banks, and also the economic and business center of Italy. The University of Milan has eight faculties and branches in COMO, CREMA, and VARESE. In recent years, the University of Milan—a sister school to BICCOCCA—was established in the northern part of Milan. The university has a total of 130 libraries, 1.07 million volumes, and 18 libraries providing audiovisual and multimedia facilities. Currently, there are over 60,000 students and 2,500 professors. In the 2002 ranking of public universities in Italy, the University of Milan ranked first in medicine, veterinary medicine, and pharmacy, second in architecture and engineering, fifth in agriculture, and sixth in science.

Specialized Settings

The University of Milan offers 77 degree programs and more than 70 graduate schools, most of which are in the fields of medicine and science and engineering. Notable faculties include the Faculty of Law, Faculty of Political Science, Faculty of Literature and Philosophy, Faculty of Science and Technology, Faculty of Agriculture, Faculty of Pharmacy, Faculty of Medicine, and Faculty of Veterinary Medicine.
