The landmines of studying abroad

In study abroad applications, some taboos from visa officers must not be touched; once touched, it is equivalent to entering a "minefield," and the application plan will end in "visa rejection." Industry experts will share professional "minefield elimination methods" to successfully avoid the "minefield."

According to Peninsula Morning Post, visa refusal is a piece of bad news that all applicants do not want to hear, and the urgent desire of each applicant to study abroad forces us to face this word. A student applying for study abroad said, "Preparing for a study abroad application is like playing a minesweeper game; every bit of effort is to avoid triggering the landmines and thus successfully crossing the border. But once you cross the visa officer's taboo, it is equivalent to failing to clear the mines and losing everything." This student's analogy makes quite a lot of sense. Some mistakes in studying abroad applications are absolutely unacceptable; once "stepping on a landmine," the consequences can be serious.

The long lead of the "landmine": material fraud

Representative countries: United Kingdom, Japan, South Korea

It is understood that in recent years, countries studying abroad have gradually gained a better understanding of China's national conditions, so their judgments of materials have become more meticulous. "In this process, embassies and consulates of various countries are deeply condemned by the phenomenon of falsification in application materials." A study abroad expert said, "Japan is a good example. In recent years, it has gradually strengthened document review and increased the frequency of telephone follow-ups. Once a student is found to have falsified information, it becomes very difficult to overturn the case." "Officials at the British Embassy also stated: If a student is found to have provided false materials in their UK student visa application, they will not be allowed to apply for a UK visa for ten years." In fact, this impact is even more far-reaching than ten years, and applicants may be affected by this shadow throughout their lifetime when applying for a UK visa. ”

A "landmine" was directly triggered: funding issues

Representative countries: United States, Canada, United Kingdom, Australia

Ultimately, the most important support for students studying abroad is funding. If a student's funds run into trouble, not only will they be unable to complete their studies, but it may also bring a series of challenges to the host country. "Clearly, countries with open education industries are very pragmatic—they are unwilling to take risks." A study abroad expert said, "Therefore, reviewing funds has become one of the most important parts of visa review." He explained that common funding issues include insufficient funds, unreasonable parental income, and unclear sources of funds. In fact, embassies and consulates do not have strict requirements for funding, but applicants must ensure that their guarantee money matches their study abroad expenses, otherwise the risk of "stepping on a landmine" is quite high.

"Landmines" can explode at any time: unreasonable study plans

Representative countries: applicable to all countries

The final review of the applicant's purpose for going abroad comes down to the study plan. As long as the student has a thorough and reasonable study plan and shows a strong desire to study, the visa officer will usually gladly approve the student. The preference for good students has always been the same, both in China and abroad. If the situation is reversed, things become much more complicated. An unreasonable or incomplete study plan will make visa officers wonder: What is the student's purpose in going abroad? Is there an immigrant tendency? Even if they study abroad, is such a student capable of completing their studies? Keep in mind, visa officers work a heavy workload, and each application may only have 3 minutes. If your study plan causes more than two questions in the visa officer's mind, your application may be rejected within 2 minutes.

留学的地雷

留学申请中，签证官的一些禁忌是触碰不得的，一旦触碰，就等于闯入了“雷区”，申请计划将会以“拒签”告终。业内人士告诉你专业的“排雷方法”，以成功避开“雷区”。

据《半岛晨报》报道，拒签，是所有留学申请者不愿听到的噩耗，而每个申请者迫切希望出国留学的愿望又让我们不得不正视这个词。一位办理留学的同学说：“准备留学申请的过程就像玩一盘扫雷游戏，每一分努力都是为了不把地雷引爆，从而成功过境。可是一旦触碰到签证官的禁忌，就等同于排雷失败，满盘皆输。”这位同学的比喻颇有些道理。留学申请中，有些错误是万万犯不得的，一旦“踩雷”后果很严重。

“地雷”的长引线：材料造假

代表国家：英国、日本、韩国

据了解，近几年来各留学国家逐渐对中国国情有了一定了解，因此在对材料的判断上也更加细致。“这个过程中，各国使领馆对申请材料中的弄虚作假现象深恶痛绝。”留学专家说道：“日本就是一个很好的例子，近几年来它逐渐加强材料审核力度，电话回访频率增高。一旦学生被发现弄虚作假，就很难再翻案了。”英国使馆的官员也表示：学生一旦在赴英留学签证的申请中被认定有提供假材料的情节，十年内不得再申请赴英签证。“而实际上，这个影响比十年更加深远，有可能申请人一生都会在赴英签证申请中受到这一阴影的影响。”

“地雷”直接拉响：资金问题

代表国家：美国、加拿大、英国、澳大利亚

说到底，学生出国留学的最重要支撑是资金，学生的资金一旦发生问题，不仅无法完成学业，更可能给接收国带来一系列难题。“很显然，教育产业开放的各个国家都很现实，他们不愿意承担风险。”留学专家说道：“因此对资金的审核成为了签证审核中最重要的部分之一。”他介绍，常见的资金问题包括资金不足、父母收入不合理、资金来源不明等等。其实使领馆对资金并没有硬性要求，但是申请者务必要使担保金与自己的留学花费相匹配，否则“踩雷”的可能性相当高。

“地雷”随时引爆：学习计划不合理

代表国家：适用于所有国家

对于申请人出国目的的审核最终还要回到学习计划上来，只要学生有周密合理的学习计划，并且表现出强烈的求学愿望，签证官通常会欣然让其通过。对于好学生的偏爱，古今中外都是一样的。事情若反过来就复杂多了，不合理不详尽的学习计划会让签证官心生疑虑：这个学生出国的目的是什么？有没有移民倾向？即使是出国学习，这样的学生有能力完成学业吗？要知道，签证官的工作强度也很大，每一份申请所能获得的时间也许只有3分钟。如果你的学习计划使签证官的头脑中产生两个以上问题，恐怕不出2分钟你的申请已经被拒了.
